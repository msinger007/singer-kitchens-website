<div class="post-body"><p>One of the most common questions we hear from homeowners is whether they should paint their existing cabinets or replace them.</p>
<p>It's a fair question — painting looks like the more affordable option, and on the surface, the results can look similar.</p>
<p>But after nearly 100 years of installing kitchens in Greater New Orleans, our designers have seen how both choices hold up over time. The difference is significant — and it almost always comes down to the finish.</p>
<p><img src="/images/2026/06/03mls.webp" alt="Why the Finish on Your Cabinets Matters More Than Most People Realize" width="1200" height="800" fetchpriority="high"><br>
<h3><strong>What's Actually Different About a Factory Finish</strong></h3><br>
When cabinets are finished in a factory, the process is controlled from start to finish. The wood is prepared, primed, and coated in a climate-controlled environment with professional spray equipment designed specifically for this purpose.</p>
<p>Multiple coats are applied with sanding and curing between each one. The result is a finish that bonds to the surface at a level that simply cannot be replicated on a job site.</p>
<p>A job-site paint application — even a skilled one — is working in a completely different environment. Dust, humidity, temperature variation, and brush or roller application all affect how the paint adheres and how it cures.</p>
<p>The New Orleans climate makes this especially pronounced. Our summers are hard on painted surfaces, and a finish that wasn't factory-applied feels it.</p>
<p><img src="/images/2026/06/compressed_2.webp" alt="Why the Finish on Your Cabinets Matters More Than Most People Realize" width="1200" height="1799" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><br>
<h3><strong>Where You See the Difference</strong></h3><br>
The gap between factory and field finishes shows up in the places that take the most wear.</p>
<p>Around handles and pulls, where hands make contact constantly. Along the edges of doors and drawer fronts, where surfaces bump into each other. Around the sink base, where moisture is a factor.</p>
<p>Factory finishes hold up in these areas because the coating has fully cured and bonded before the cabinet was ever installed.</p>
<p>Field-painted cabinets are more vulnerable to chipping, peeling, and yellowing in exactly these spots — often within a few years, sometimes sooner.</p>
<p>We hear from homeowners regularly who painted their cabinets two or three years ago and are already unhappy with how they look. That's a frustrating outcome when replacement was the better long-term decision from the start.</p>
<p><img src="/images/2026/06/10-2-1.webp" alt="Why the Finish on Your Cabinets Matters More Than Most People Realize" width="1200" height="1800" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><br>
<h3><strong>What This Means for Your Decision</strong></h3><br>
If your cabinets are structurally sound and the layout works well for how you cook and live, painting may still make sense in some situations. Our designers will tell you honestly when that's the case.</p>
<p>But if you're painting because you're not happy with the cabinets — the look, the storage, the way they function — replacement gives you the opportunity to fix all of those things at once, with a finish that will hold up the way the originals never did.</p>
<p>The goal is a kitchen you still love five years from now. The finish is a bigger part of that than most people expect when they're making the decision.<br>
